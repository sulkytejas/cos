›1The skeletonthe app and the server speakshipped

The bones everything else stands on. The phone app and the server share one truth: whatever happens on one side shows up on the other within seconds. The server keeps the real records; the phone keeps a fast mirror so every screen opens instantly, even offline. A night worker runs on the server and does the heavy thinking so your phone never has to.

This is done and battle-tested. Crashes recover cleanly, the same action sent twice never files twice, and the phone pairs with a token only you hold.

already true — open the app, everything is live data, nothing is hardcoded.
›2The morningsshe works the night, you read a lettershipped

Every night the worker reads what arrived — mail, meetings, documents — prepares briefs for anything that deserves preparation, and writes you one morning letter: a verdict on the day, a short memo, and everything it held for your decision.

The review is now a single written page. You mark it with one pen — strike any word or any whole thought — and the review ends when you put the letter down: struck things are declined, untouched things quietly file themselves, and the page collapses to a small "KEPT" stamp until tomorrow. No buttons, no card stacks, no approve-all.

already true — this is today's Review screen.
›3Memoryshe keeps a notebook, and she bets on itshipped

Ayumi keeps a notebook. Each night it writes a few small notes about your world — who runs late, what someone keeps pushing on, what changed — every note backed by the actual emails that prove it. Wrong notes get end-dated, never deleted, so a forgotten thing can never sneak back. Your strike on a memo line reaches all the way down and makes the note behind it forgotten.

On top of the notebook sits the part nobody else has: predictions. Each night Ayumi places a few small bets — "Karan replies by Thursday, 78% sure" — and reality grades them. Right bets strengthen the notes they came from; wrong bets weaken them. There's an honest running score, and when it's been overconfident, it starts hedging exactly there.

Proven with a full three-week rehearsal: notes appeared with receipts, junk was rejected, a contradiction corrected itself, a bet was graded, a strike made something stay forgotten.

Saved for later, with written triggers: notes that fade if life stops confirming them · a weekly "who you are" page · a per-topic scorecard · noticing when what you say and what you do disagree.

already true — "Karan at 14:30 is prepared — cohort curve first, twenty real minutes, not thirty."
›4Talking back — finishing the home screenthe thread becomes a conversationbuild next

Today's home screen is half done. The half that works: you wake up to a real morning letter, see the brief cards, strike the memo, get nudged to connect a missing source. The half that's missing: you can't talk back. The thread looks like a conversation but only one side ever speaks.

4.1
The reply railType under any turn and Ayumi answers right there in the thread, with the notebook in hand — not a separate chat screen, the same page. While it thinks, a small transient "reading the deck…" beat shows, then dissolves into the answer.
4.2
Ghost repliesAyumi already knows the two things you're most likely to say back — so it writes them ahead of you, as faint ink under its message. Tap one and it sends. Most mornings you never type at all. This replaces the compose box as the main way of answering.
4.3
Margin notesPress a single sentence in any turn and reply to exactly that sentence — the answer comes back anchored to it, like writing in a book's margin. Same idea as the word-level pen, applied to conversation.
4.4
Held questions surface as turnsWhen Ayumi held something overnight that genuinely needs your answer, the question itself appears as its own turn in the thread — with its two answers as tappable words — instead of hiding in a queue.
you reply "push it to Friday" under the morning letter and Ayumi answers in-thread ten seconds later, having actually moved the thing — and most days you answer by tapping ghost ink, not typing.
›5The voicehold, speak, it's filedto build

The capture well already works for typing: half a thought in, Ayumi completes it, files it under the right chapter. The voice path is currently a scripted demo — it plays three hardcoded lines and no real listening happens.

5.1
Real speech-to-textApple's on-device recognizer (nothing leaves the phone for transcription). Hold the well, talk naturally, watch your words appear as you say them.
5.2
Same completion brainThe transcript flows into the exact same completion → classification → filing path the typed capture already uses. No new machinery — just a new mouth.
walking out of a meeting you hold the well and say "Karan wants the cohort table before Thursday" — by the time you've pocketed the phone it's a todo under Stratyfix.
›6The notebook room"what Ayumi knows" — read its mind, strike its beliefswith designer

The only screen that shows Ayumi's beliefs instead of its conclusions. The cheat sheet it carries into every thought, the open bets with their honest score, then the notebook grouped by person — every note one plain sentence with its proof one tap away. Strike a note and it's forgotten; nothing is ever editable, only forgettable.

You reach it three ways: through the avatar's nav menu like any room; by pressing a person's name anywhere (opens at their page); or by tapping "person notes" in a letter's source line (opens at the evidence).

The full designer brief is written — layout, idiom rules, states, what it must never become. Build starts when the design lands.

you press "Karan" in the morning letter and see the five things Ayumi believes about him, how often life confirmed each one, and the emails that prove them.
›7Real lifeyour real mail, your real phone, a real 6amdecision parked

Everything so far runs against rehearsal data on a laptop. This phase moves Ayumi to a small rented server (about ₹400/month) with a proper address, so three things become real at once:

7.1
Your real phone connectsThe app only talks over secure connections, which a laptop can't offer the outside world. A real server can — pair once, carry Ayumi in your pocket.
7.2
Your real Gmail, Calendar and DriveThe connect buttons already exist and the plumbing is written — Google just refuses to talk to a laptop. With a public address, tapping CONNECT GMAIL actually works, and Ayumi reads your real correspondence (passwords never stored, tokens encrypted).
7.3
The 6am is a real 6amThe night worker runs while you sleep instead of when we poke it. Plus automatic backups of the database every few minutes.

Why parked: it's a one-time ~2-hour setup but needs your accounts (server, domain, Google console). The moment it happens, Ayumi stops being a thing we build and becomes a thing you live with.

you wake up at 7, open your own phone, and the letter is about your actual day — written at 6 while you slept.
›8Eyes and handsshe can look things up and do thingslater
8.1
Web eyesRight now if Ayumi wants to check something on the internet, the tool politely returns nothing. Real search and page-reading lets briefs cite the outside world — "their fund announced a new partner yesterday."
8.2
Living chapters for everythingThe North India trip page — the one that corrects itself when you say "no, I took the bus" — is the pattern. Apply it beyond the demo trip, so any chapter can grow its own living page from its palette.
8.3
Doing, not just draftingToday Ayumi drafts replies and todos for you to send. The far end of this phase is sending on your standing instruction — only ever where you've explicitly said "things like this, just send."
a brief that quotes yesterday's news next to your own mail, and a chapter page that builds itself.
